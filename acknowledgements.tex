\begin{acknowledgements} 	 
    In the winter of 2018, I was at a fork in the road for my career.
    I was finishing up my Bachelor's degree and worked a few internships and contracting, and did a little research at school.
    I really wasn't sure what I wanted to work on, if I wanted to focus on something more academic, or even if I wanted to work on hardware versus software.
    Around this time, I was looking for something to do that summer and was shown a posting for a summer job at Caltech working with Sandy Weinreb on radio astronomy.
    At this point, I knew nothing about the field apart from I seemed to have the ``hands on'' radio experience that Sandy wanted, so I emailed him.
    Less than five minutes later (which would have been close to midnight in California), I got a response asking if I could FaceTime him.
    We talked for over two hours.
    That summer I worked with Sandy and the team on hardware that would become part of the DSA-10 radio telescope, and I have never had so much fun.
    As such, I would first and foremost like to thank Sandy for introducing me to this field and providing invaluable mentorship over these years, forever altering the trajectory of my career.

    A few years later, after I finished my Master’s degree, I was accepted to come back to Caltech to work on a PhD.
